## Slide 3: The Three Narrative Tenses 📖
### An Overview

When telling a story in English, three past tenses work together to create depth and detail.

| Tense | Main Use | Signal Words |
|---|---|---|
| **Past Simple** | Main events — what happened | yesterday, then, after that, suddenly, finally |
| **Past Continuous** | Background actions — what was already happening | while, when, at that moment, at the time |
| **Past Perfect** | Events before the story — what had already happened | before, already, by the time, after, because |

**Quick illustration:**

> *She **was walking** home when she **heard** a strange sound.*
> *She **had never heard** anything like it before.*

- **was walking** = background action (Past Continuous)
- **heard** = main event (Past Simple)
- **had never heard** = event before the story moment (Past Perfect)

## Slide 4: Past Simple — The Backbone of a Story 🦴
### The Main Events

The **Past Simple** carries the main actions of a story. It moves the plot forward.

**Structure:** Subject + verb (past form)

| Regular Verbs | Irregular Verbs |
|---|---|
| *She **walked** into the room.* | *He **saw** something unusual.* |
| *They **called** the police.* | *She **ran** as fast as she could.* |
| *I **decided** to stay home.* | *We **left** before midnight.* |

**Story sequence example:**

> *First, she **opened** the door. Then, she **walked** inside. After that, she **noticed** that something was wrong. Finally, she **called** her friend.*

> 💡 Use **Past Simple** every time you describe the next event in the sequence.

---

## Slide 5: Past Continuous — The Background 🎬
### What Was Already Happening

The **Past Continuous** sets the scene. It describes an ongoing action that was already in progress when something else happened.

**Structure:** Subject + was / were + verb-ing

| Setting the Scene | Example |
|---|---|
| weather | *It **was raining** heavily outside.* |
| people's actions | *Everyone **was talking** at the party.* |
| mood / atmosphere | *The children **were playing** in the garden.* |

**Interruption pattern — Past Continuous + Past Simple:**

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **While** + Past Continuous, Past Simple | *While she **was reading**, the phone **rang**.* |
| Past Simple + **when** + Past Continuous | *He **arrived** when they **were still waiting**.* |

> 💡 The Past Continuous describes what was **already in progress**.  
> The Past Simple describes what **interrupted or happened next**.

## Slide 6: Past Simple vs Past Continuous — Side by Side ⚖️
### Choosing the Right Tense

| Situation | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A completed action at a specific time | Past Simple | *She **left** at 10 o'clock.* |
| An action in progress at a specific time | Past Continuous | *At 10 o'clock, she **was leaving** the building.* |
| A sequence of events | Past Simple | *He **opened** the window and **looked** outside.* |
| Background description | Past Continuous | *The sun **was setting** and the birds **were singing**.* |
| Interrupted action | Past Continuous + Past Simple | *I **was cooking** when the fire alarm **went off**.* |

**Common contrast:**

| ❌ Less natural | ✅ More natural |
|---|---|
| *While she drove, the car stopped.* | *While she **was driving**, the car **stopped**.* |
| *When he arrived, she cooked.* | *When he **arrived**, she **was cooking**.* |

## Slide 7: Past Perfect — Before the Story 🕰️
### What Had Already Happened

The **Past Perfect** describes an action that happened **before** another past event.  
It gives context and explains why something happened.

**Structure:** Subject + had + past participle

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Action before another past event | *She **had left** before he **arrived**.* |
| Explaining the cause of a situation | *He **was** tired because he **hadn't slept** well.* |
| Background information | *They **had never met** before that night.* |

**Sequence logic:**

> *By the time the police arrived, the thief **had already escaped**.*

- First: the thief escaped → **Past Perfect**
- Second: the police arrived → **Past Simple**

**Common signal words for Past Perfect:**

| Word / Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| **already** | *She had already left when he called.* |
| **by the time** | *By the time we arrived, the movie had started.* |
| **before** | *He had eaten before the guests arrived.* |
| **after** | *After they had finished, they went home.* |
| **never / ever** | *I had never seen anything like it.* |

> 💡 You do not always need the Past Perfect. Use it when the order of events needs to be made clear.

## Slide 8: Time Sequencers ⏱️
### Connecting Events in Order

Time sequencers organize events clearly. They show the reader or listener what happened first, next, and last.

**Beginning the story:**

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| **Once** | *Once, a young traveler arrived in a strange town.* |
| **One day** | *One day, she found a letter in the attic.* |
| **It all started when...** | *It all started when he missed his train.* |
| **At first** | *At first, everything seemed normal.* |

**During the story:**

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| **Then** | *Then, she noticed the window was open.* |
| **After that** | *After that, things moved quickly.* |
| **Meanwhile** | *Meanwhile, her friends were still waiting outside.* |
| **Suddenly** | *Suddenly, the lights went out.* |
| **At that moment** | *At that moment, she realized the truth.* |
| **A few minutes later** | *A few minutes later, the police arrived.* |

**Ending the story:**

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| **Eventually** | *Eventually, they found a solution.* |
| **In the end** | *In the end, she decided to stay.* |
| **Finally** | *Finally, he opened the letter.* |
| **After all that** | *After all that, they went home exhausted.* |

## Slide 9: Story Linking Words 🔗
### Connecting Ideas and Clauses

Linking words help explain cause and effect, contrast, and the reason events happen.

**Cause and Effect:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | explains reason | *She was scared because the door was locked.* |
| **so** | shows result | *It was raining, so they stayed inside.* |
| **as a result** | formal result | *The storm arrived early. As a result, the event was cancelled.* |
| **that's why** | explains consequence | *He had no money. That's why he didn't go.* |

**Contrast and Surprise:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **but** | simple contrast | *She wanted to leave, but she couldn't.* |
| **however** | formal contrast | *It seemed safe. However, there was a hidden danger.* |
| **although** | contrast within a clause | *Although she was tired, she kept walking.* |
| **unexpectedly** | surprise turn | *Unexpectedly, he appeared at the door.* |

**Addition:**

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **also** | adds information | *The road was dark. It was also quiet.* |
| **besides** | adds extra point | *Besides, no one knew where they were going.* |
| **what's more** | emphasizes addition | *The town was small. What's more, it had no hospital.* |

## Slide 10: Story Structure 📋
### Beginning, Middle, and End

A well-told story has three parts. Each part uses different vocabulary and tenses.

**Part 1 — Setting the scene (Beginning)**

> Introduce the **characters**, the **place**, and the **situation**.  
> Use Past Continuous for background + Past Simple for the first event.

*It was a cold winter evening. The streets **were empty** and the wind **was blowing** hard. A woman named Clara **was walking** home alone when she suddenly **heard** footsteps behind her.*

---

**Part 2 — The problem / action (Middle)**

> Develop the **conflict** or main action.  
> Use Past Simple for sequence + time sequencers.

*She **turned** around, but no one **was there**. Then, she **noticed** a shadow near the wall. Suddenly, a cat **jumped** out of a bin and **ran** across the street. Clara **laughed** at herself and **continued** walking.*

---

**Part 3 — The resolution (End)**

> Show how the story **resolves**.  
> Use eventually, in the end, finally + Past Simple.

*Eventually, she **arrived** home safe and warm. By the time she **made** a cup of tea, she **had already forgotten** about the scare. In the end, it **was** just a quiet evening — nothing more.*

## Slide 11: Phrasal Verbs for Storytelling 📘
### New Expressions to Enrich Your Narrative

These phrasal verbs appear frequently in stories, conversations about past events, and personal narratives.

| Phrasal Verb | Translation | Example in Context |
|---|---|---|
| **end up** | acabar em / terminar de determinada forma | *They ended up sleeping on the floor.* |
| **turn out** | resultar em / descobrir que | *It turned out that no one had left.* |
| **come across** | encontrar por acaso | *She came across an old photo in the drawer.* |
| **go through** | passar por (algo difícil) | *He went through a very difficult year.* |
| **make up** | inventar (história/mentira) | *She made up an excuse and left early.* |
| **carry on** | continuar | *Despite the rain, they carried on walking.* |
| **break down** | desmoronar / perder o controle emocional | *He broke down when he heard the news.* |
| **grow up** | crescer / ter uma criação | *She grew up in a small village in the mountains.* |
| **set out** | partir com objetivo / começar uma jornada | *They set out early in the morning.* |
| **fall apart** | desmoronar / se desfazer | *Without a leader, the team began to fall apart.* |
| **bring up** | mencionar / criar (um filho) | *No one brought up the argument from the night before.* |
| **pass away** | falecer (forma mais suave) | *His grandfather had passed away the year before.* |

> 💡 Notice that **"turn out"** and **"end up"** are especially common in story endings and conclusions.  
> *It turned out that she had been right all along.*  
> *They ended up becoming best friends.*

## Slide 12: Phrasal Verbs — Multiple Contexts 🔄
### The Same Verb, Different Situations

**End up:**

| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| Unexpected result | *We took the wrong road and ended up in the next town.* |
| Final state | *After hours of arguing, they ended up agreeing.* |
| Unplanned situation | *He ended up staying for three weeks.* |

**Turn out:**

| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| Result was different from expected | *It turned out to be a great night.* |
| Discovering the truth | *It turned out that she had been there the whole time.* |
| Used with "well" / "badly" | *Fortunately, everything turned out well.* |

**Come across:**

| Context | Example |
|---|---|
| Find by chance | *I came across his name in an old newspaper.* |
| Give an impression | *She came across as calm and confident.* |

> 💡 **"Come across as"** (causar uma impressão) is a very useful expression for describing characters:
> *The villain came across as charming at first.*

## Slide 13: Formal vs Everyday Narrative Language 🎩👕
### Telling Stories in Different Registers

| Everyday (Spoken) | More Formal / Literary | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| *She ended up in trouble.* | *She found herself in a difficult situation.* | acabou em apuros |
| *He made up a story.* | *He fabricated an account.* | inventou uma história |
| *Things fell apart.* | *The situation deteriorated rapidly.* | as coisas desmoronaram |
| *She came across a letter.* | *She discovered / encountered a letter.* | encontrou uma carta |
| *He went through a lot.* | *He endured a great deal of hardship.* | passou por muito |
| *They carried on.* | *They persevered / continued their journey.* | continuaram |

> 💡 In personal storytelling (talking to friends), everyday language sounds more natural.  
> In written stories or formal accounts, more literary vocabulary may be preferred.

## Slide 14: Common Mistakes 🚫
### Errors with Narrative Tenses

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| *While she drove, the car stopped.* | *While she **was driving**, the car stopped.* | Background actions use Past Continuous |
| *When I arrived, she cooked.* | *When I arrived, she **was cooking**.* | She was already cooking — use Past Continuous |
| *She left before he arrived.* (when order matters) | *She **had left** before he arrived.* | Past Perfect shows which event came first |
| *I ended up to stay at home.* | *I ended up **staying** at home.* | "End up" is followed by **-ing** |
| *It turned out to be not what I expected.* | *It turned out **not to be** what I expected.* | Negation position in infinitive |
| *He come across a solution.* | *He **came across** a solution.* | Use past form of the main verb |
| *Suddenly he is gone.* | *Suddenly he **was gone**.* | Use past tense in a narrative |
| *They setted out at dawn.* | *They **set out** at dawn.* | "Set" is irregular — past form is **set** |

## Slide 15: Putting It All Together 🌱
### A Short Story Using Narrative Tenses

**Context:** A woman tells a story about an unexpected night.

---

> *One evening, Clara **was sitting** alone in her flat. The rain **was falling** quietly outside.*
>
> *She **had been** home all day and **had finished** her work early. Suddenly, her phone **rang**. It **was** an old friend she **hadn't spoken** to in years.*
>
> *"Come to the café," her friend **said**. "I need to talk to you."*
>
> *Clara **hesitated**, but **decided** to go. She **put on** her coat and **set out** into the rain.*
>
> *When she **arrived**, her friend **was sitting** at a corner table. He **looked** nervous. He **brought up** something she **had never expected** to hear — he **had found** the letter she **had written** ten years before but **had never sent**.*
>
> *She **went through** a rush of emotions. She **didn't know** what to say.*
>
> *Eventually, they **talked** for hours. In the end, it **turned out** to be one of the best conversations she **had ever had**.*
>
> *She **ended up** walking home in the rain — but this time, she **was smiling**.*

---

**Notice the narrative tools used:**
- ✅ **Past Continuous** for the scene: *was sitting, was falling*
- ✅ **Past Perfect** for background: *had been, hadn't spoken, had never sent*
- ✅ **Past Simple** for main events: *rang, decided, arrived, talked*
- ✅ **Time sequencers**: *suddenly, when, eventually, in the end*
- ✅ **Phrasal verbs**: *put on, set out, brought up, turned out, ended up*
- ✅ **Linking words**: *but, so*
