## Slide 3: What Is the Passive Voice? 🔄
### Focus on the Action, Not the Person

In the **active voice**, the subject does the action.

> *The manager closed the office.*

In the **passive voice**, the subject receives the action.

> *The office was closed.*

The passive is common when:

- the action is more important than the person
- the person is unknown
- the person is obvious
- the language is formal, neutral, or impersonal

**Compare:**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *Someone stole my bike.* | *My bike was stolen.* |
| *The company makes the phones in China.* | *The phones are made in China.* |
| *The school announced the results this morning.* | *The results were announced this morning.* |

> 💡 In public communication, English often prefers the passive because it sounds objective and neutral.

## Slide 4: Passive Structure in the Present ✅
### Am / Is / Are + Past Participle

**Basic structure:**

> subject + **am / is / are** + **past participle**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *They clean the rooms every day.* | *The rooms are cleaned every day.* |
| *People speak English here.* | *English is spoken here.* |
| *They sell tickets online.* | *Tickets are sold online.* |

**Negative form:**

> *Pets are not allowed inside.*

**Question form:**

> *Is breakfast served in the hotel?*

**Common present passive contexts:**

- rules
- signs
- instructions
- product descriptions
- formal announcements

---

## Slide 5: Passive Structure in the Past 🕰️
### Was / Were + Past Participle

**Basic structure:**

> subject + **was / were** + **past participle**

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *The police found the missing child.* | *The missing child was found.* |
| *They built the bridge in 1998.* | *The bridge was built in 1998.* |
| *The teacher explained the rules yesterday.* | *The rules were explained yesterday.* |

**Negative form:**

> *The package wasn't delivered on time.*

**Question form:**

> *Were the documents sent last night?*

**Common past passive contexts:**

- simple news reports
- historical facts
- completed procedures
- public updates

---

## Slide 6: The Agent with "By" 👤
### When the Doer Matters

Sometimes we include the person or thing that did the action.  
We introduce that information with **by**.

> *The song was written **by** Adele.*  
> *The museum was designed **by** a famous architect.*

Use **by + agent** when:

- the doer is important
- the doer is interesting
- the doer adds necessary information

Do **not** use **by** when:

- the doer is unknown
- the doer is obvious
- the doer is not important

**Compare:**

| Passive Sentence | Why? |
|---|---|
| *My wallet was stolen.* | the thief is unknown |
| *The road was closed.* | the authority is obvious / not important |
| *The novel was written by George Orwell.* | the writer is important |

> 💡 Many beginner mistakes happen when students add **by** to every passive sentence. Most of the time, it is not necessary.

## Slide 7: Passive in Notices and Signs 🚫
### Short, Clear, Impersonal Language

The passive voice is extremely common in notices, warnings, and signs because the message needs to be clear and direct.

**Examples:**

| Notice / Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Smoking is prohibited.** | Fumar e proibido. |
| **Visitors are not allowed beyond this point.** | Visitantes nao podem passar deste ponto. |
| **Bags must be checked at the entrance.** | As bolsas devem ser verificadas na entrada. |
| **Payment is required before entry.** | O pagamento e exigido antes da entrada. |
| **The museum is closed on Mondays.** | O museu fecha as segundas-feiras. |
| **Reservations are recommended.** | Reservas sao recomendadas. |

Why passive works well here:

- it sounds formal
- it removes the personal subject
- it focuses on the rule, not on who created it

## Slide 8: Passive in Instructions and Processes ⚙️
### Explaining How Things Are Done

The passive is also common when we describe a process step by step.

**Examples:**

| Process Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *The ingredients are mixed in a large bowl.* | Os ingredientes sao misturados em uma tigela grande. |
| *The form is completed online.* | O formulario e preenchido online. |
| *The emails are checked every morning.* | Os emails sao verificados toda manha. |
| *The product was tested before release.* | O produto foi testado antes do lancamento. |
| *The documents were scanned and stored digitally.* | Os documentos foram escaneados e armazenados digitalmente. |

Passive is common in:

- recipes
- manuals
- formal procedures
- work instructions
- product descriptions

> 💡 In process descriptions, the person is often not important. The steps are the focus.

## Slide 9: Passive in News and Public Reports 📰
### Objective and Neutral Tone

Simple news and public reports often use the passive to sound neutral and factual.

**Examples:**

| Sentence | Focus |
|---|---|
| *Two people were injured in the accident.* | result of the event |
| *The new policy was announced yesterday.* | public information |
| *Several schools were closed because of the storm.* | official update |
| *The missing dog was found near the station.* | outcome |
| *A new hospital is being planned in the region.* | public development |

Why journalists and institutions use the passive:

- to emphasize facts
- to avoid unnecessary personal focus
- to keep the tone neutral
- to report incomplete information carefully

## Slide 10: Active vs Passive Choice ⚖️
### When Should You Use Each One?

Both active and passive are correct, but they create different focus.

| Active | Passive | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| *The company launched the app last month.* | *The app was launched last month.* | active = company, passive = app |
| *The chef prepares the meals every morning.* | *The meals are prepared every morning.* | active = chef, passive = meals |
| *The police arrested the suspect.* | *The suspect was arrested.* | active = police, passive = suspect |

Use the **active** voice when:

- the doer is important
- the sentence should feel more direct
- the subject is the real focus

Use the **passive** voice when:

- the action or result matters more
- the doer is unknown or obvious
- the style should sound more formal or impersonal

## Slide 11: Common Mistakes 🚫
### Errors Brazilian Students Often Make

**1. Forgetting the verb "be"**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| *The food prepared every day.* | *The food is prepared every day.* |
| *The message sent yesterday.* | *The message was sent yesterday.* |

**2. Using the past tense instead of the past participle**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| *The car was stole.* | *The car was stolen.* |
| *The file was send.* | *The file was sent.* |

**3. Confusing active and passive order**

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| *By the company made the product.* | *The product was made by the company.* |
| *The rules explained the teacher.* | *The rules were explained by the teacher.* |

**4. Using passive when active is clearer**

| Less natural | More natural |
|---|---|
| *My brother was called me.* | *My brother called me.* |
| *I was eaten breakfast at 7.* | *I ate breakfast at 7.* |

> 💡 Not every sentence should be passive. Choose it only when the focus needs to be on the action or the result.

## Slide 12: Present Passive and Past Passive Overview 🧠
### Quick Summary

| Time | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present passive | **am / is / are + past participle** | *The office is cleaned every day.* |
| Past passive | **was / were + past participle** | *The office was cleaned yesterday.* |
| Passive with agent | **be + past participle + by** | *The office was cleaned by the staff.* |

**Very common passive verbs in public English:**

- made
- built
- sold
- served
- announced
- delivered
- produced
- closed
- allowed
- required
- printed
- published

## Slide 13: Public English in Real Contexts 🌍
### Where Students Will Actually See This

Students often meet the passive voice in real life in places such as:

- airports: *Boarding is delayed.*
- hotels: *Breakfast is served from 6 to 10.*
- hospitals: *Visitors are not permitted after 9 p.m.*
- apps and websites: *Your password is required.*
- packaging: *Made in Brazil.*
- schools: *Final results will be published on Friday.*
- news sites: *The road was reopened this morning.*

This is why the passive is not only grammar.  
It is also a reading skill for understanding public messages quickly and correctly.

---

## Slide 14: Final Takeaways 🌟
### What Matters Most

- The passive voice focuses on the action, result, or object
- Present passive uses **am / is / are + past participle**
- Past passive uses **was / were + past participle**
- Use **by** only when the agent is important
- Passive structures are very common in signs, notices, formal instructions, products, and news
- Clear reading of public English depends a lot on recognizing passive patterns

> English in public spaces often sounds more impersonal than Portuguese.  
> The passive voice is one of the main reasons why.
