## Slide 5 — Grammar Focus 1: Present Simple × Present Continuous

### Present Simple
> Used for **permanent habits**, **routines**, and **general truths**.

- *I go to the gym three times a week.*
- *She doesn't eat red meat.*
- *They usually walk to work.*

---

### Present Continuous
> Used for **temporary situations** and **changes happening now**.

- *I'm going to the gym more often lately.* *(temporary change)*
- *She's trying to cut back on sugar.* *(in progress)*
- *He's working from home this month.* *(current situation)*

---

## Slide 6 — Present Simple × Present Continuous: Side by Side

| Situation | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent routine | Present Simple | *I wake up at 7 a.m. every day.* |
| Temporary change | Present Continuous | *I'm waking up earlier this week.* |
| General preference | Present Simple | *She prefers tea to coffee.* |
| Evolving habit | Present Continuous | *She's drinking less coffee these days.* |
| Fact / truth | Present Simple | *Exercise improves mood.* |
| Ongoing action | Present Continuous | *He's reading a lot about nutrition lately.* |

## Slide 7 — Grammar Focus 2: Stative Verbs

### What are stative verbs?
Verbs that describe **states** (feelings, thoughts, perceptions, possession) — NOT actions.

> ⚠️ Stative verbs are **NOT normally used in continuous tenses**.

---

### Categories of Stative Verbs

| Category | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Emotions & feelings | love, hate, like, prefer, fear, adore, mind |
| Mental states | know, believe, think*, understand, remember, forget, realize |
| Senses | see, hear, smell*, taste*, feel* |
| Possession | have*, own, belong, possess, contain |
| Appearance / being | seem, appear, look*, be*, cost, weigh |

> *Some verbs have both stative and dynamic uses — context determines the meaning.

---

## Slide 8 — Stative vs Dynamic: Same Verb, Different Meaning

| Stative (state) | Dynamic (action) |
|---|---|
| *I **think** she's right.* (= opinion) | *I'**m thinking** about changing jobs.* (= process) |
| *It **smells** amazing.* (= perception) | *She'**s smelling** the flowers.* (= deliberate action) |
| *He **has** a car.* (= possession) | *He'**s having** lunch.* (= activity) |
| *The soup **tastes** good.* (= perception) | *She'**s tasting** the soup.* (= testing it) |
| *You **look** tired.* (= appearance) | *She'**s looking** at you.* (= directing eyes) |

## Slide 9 — Grammar Focus 3: Advanced Frequency Adverbs

### Basic (review)
> always — usually — often — sometimes — rarely — never

---

### Advanced Frequency Expressions

| Expression | Meaning / Use |
|---|---|
| hardly ever | almost never — *I hardly ever check email at night.* |
| every now and then | occasionally — *Every now and then, I cook a big meal.* |
| from time to time | sometimes, irregularly — *From time to time, she works overtime.* |
| once in a while | occasionally — *Once in a while, we go hiking.* |
| on and off | not regularly, with interruptions — *I've been running on and off.* |
| more often than not | usually, most of the time — *More often than not, I skip breakfast.* |
| at times | sometimes, in certain moments — *At times, I feel overwhelmed.* |

---

## Slide 10 — Adverb Placement Rules

### With the main verb:
> Subject + adverb + main verb

- *She **rarely** eats fast food.*
- *I **hardly ever** watch TV before bed.*

---

### With "be" or auxiliary verbs:
> Subject + be/aux + adverb

- *He **is always** late.*
- *They **have never** tried sushi.*

---

### At the beginning or end (for emphasis):
- ***Every now and then**, I take a digital detox.*
- *I take a digital detox **every now and then**.*

---

## Slide 11 — Grammar Focus 4: "Tend to"

### Structure:
> **Subject + tend to + base verb**

Used to describe **typical behavior**, **general inclinations**, or **patterns** — softer and more natural than "always".

---

### Examples:
- *I **tend to** overthink decisions.*
- *She **tends to** avoid conflict.*
- *We **tend to** eat late on weekdays.*
- *He doesn't **tend to** complain much.*
- *Do you **tend to** stay up late?*

---

### "Tend to" vs "usually":
| | Example |
|---|---|
| **usually** | *I usually have coffee in the morning.* *(neutral routine)* |
| **tend to** | *I tend to rely on coffee when I'm stressed.* *(pattern/inclination)* |

> **"Tend to"** implies a natural or psychological pattern — not just a timed habit.

---

## Slide 12 — Putting It All Together

> **How to describe yourself at B1:**

*"I **tend to** be quite active during the week. I **usually** go to the gym, but lately I've **been trying** to walk more instead. I **don't tend to** watch much TV — I **prefer** reading. **Every now and then**, I **take** a break from my routine and just relax. I **believe** balance is really important."*

---

### What this paragraph shows:
- ✅ Present Simple for routine (*go*, *don't tend to*, *prefer*)
- ✅ Present Continuous for current change (*have been trying*)
- ✅ Stative verb correctly used (*prefer*, *believe*)
- ✅ Advanced frequency adverb (*every now and then*)
- ✅ "Tend to" for inclination

---

## Slide 13 — Key Grammar Summary

| Structure | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | permanent habits, facts | *She meditates every morning.* |
| Present Continuous | temporary/evolving actions | *I'm meditating more this month.* |
| Stative verb | state, not action — no `-ing` | *I love yoga.* ✅ / ~~I'm loving yoga.~~ ❌ |
| Frequency adverb | how often | *I hardly ever skip sleep.* |
| tend to + verb | natural inclination | *I tend to procrastinate.* |

## Slide 14 — Vocabulary Review: Collocations

> Common collocations with **lifestyle & habits**:

- **maintain** a healthy lifestyle
- **develop** a habit / a routine
- **break** a bad habit
- **follow** a strict diet / schedule
- **adopt** a new approach / habit
- **stick to** a routine / plan
- **fall into** a habit / pattern
- **struggle with** a habit / change
- **make** progress / changes
